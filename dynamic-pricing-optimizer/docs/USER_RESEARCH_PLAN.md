# User Research Plan

**Status:** research plan and hypothesis set. No interviews have been conducted as part of this repository. Do not report these assumptions as validated findings.

## Research question
How do retail pricing decision-makers combine competitor prices, demand and inventory signals, and what evidence do they need before approving a price change?

## Participants
Recruit 5–8 people across roles, ideally 2 pricing/e-commerce analysts, 2 category or merchandising managers, 1 finance/operations reviewer, and 1–3 small online sellers. Include a mix of experience levels and product categories.

## Method
- 20–30 minute semi-structured interviews.
- Ask participants to walk through a recent pricing decision, using a sanitised example if business data is sensitive.
- Follow with a 10-minute usability test of the prototype using three tasks.
- Obtain consent, avoid collecting confidential pricing data, and anonymise notes.

## Interview guide
1. Tell me about the last time you changed a product price.
2. Which signals did you consult and where did each signal come from?
3. How often do you review prices? What causes a review?
4. What makes competitor prices difficult to compare fairly?
5. How do you decide when to protect margin versus clear inventory?
6. What could go wrong if a suggested price were followed automatically?
7. Which guardrails or approvals are mandatory in your organisation?
8. What evidence would make you trust or reject a recommendation?
9. How do you measure whether a price change worked?
10. What is missing from this prototype that would prevent you using it?

## Usability tasks
- Find the SKU with the weakest demand signal.
- Inspect a recommendation and explain the three factors that influenced it.
- Export the catalogue recommendations for review.

Observe completion, time, errors, confidence (1–5) and points of confusion. Do not lead the participant to the answer.

## Hypotheses to validate
- Analysts currently consolidate at least two signals manually.
- Users value an explanation and editable guardrails more than an opaque “AI score”.
- A clear stock-cover indicator helps identify excess inventory faster than raw units alone.
- Finance reviewers want a minimum-margin floor visible before approval.
- Users will not trust competitor prices without source, timestamp and comparable-product context.

## Synthesis template
For each participant record: role, workflow, trigger, data sources, pain point, workaround, risk, quote (anonymised), severity, frequency and product implication. Group observations into themes, separate direct evidence from interpretation, then prioritise by user impact and frequency.

## Ethics and privacy
Request permission before recording. Do not collect credentials, customer PII, confidential competitor contracts or proprietary price lists. Use anonymised notes and delete raw recordings according to the agreed retention plan.
